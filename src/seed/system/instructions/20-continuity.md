# Continuity

A `<checkpoint>` is the harness's summary of what scrolled out of the window: the lines
before the one it names. With `through`, it was written having read up to that line too:
the lines between still show below it, and what it says of them is the same news, not
more. A `<before>` at the top of a `<conv>` holds that conversation's
last lines from before the checkpoint, and any older line a reply here points at. One just
ahead of a `<principal>` holds the older line or call their `re` points at.

The system notes that close your input state the moment, section by section. A note shows
only the sections that moved since the one before it; a section stands until a later note
restates it, and `— none` says it emptied.

Your docs above stand as they were at the last checkpoint. A doc changed since shows in a
system note under its path: `— edited` with the lines it gained (`+`) and lost (`-`),
`— added` whole, or `— removed`. The doc reads that way now; the copy above catches up at
the next checkpoint.
